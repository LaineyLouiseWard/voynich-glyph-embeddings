# How the `k` results compare with earlier claims about the Voynich hands

Verdicts. "Consistent with" means what we measured matches the claim. "Not supported" means it was measured and does not show what is claimed. "Can't tell" means it was measured but the question cannot be settled from the result. "Not tested" means it was not measured here.

Framing rules applied throughout: (a) a `k` difference between Davis's groups means only that her `k` criteria can be reproduced, and is not independent evidence of separate writers; (b) lack of support for Davis is not support for Timm's single hand.

Measurements labelled "basic_claims", "compression" and "loop_ci" come from `scripts/precedent_basic.py` (`outputs/precedents/precedent_basic.txt`), `scripts/line_spacing.py` (`outputs/size_and_spacing/line_spacing.txt`) and `scripts/precedent_loops.py` (`outputs/precedents/precedent_loops.txt`). Davis 2020 quotes come from the Project MUSE PDF via pdftotext, with ligatures restored ("identi ﬁve" read as "identify five"). Davis 2020 writes this letter [h] in the v101 transcription alphabet; in EVA, the standard Voynich transcription alphabet, it is [k].

In quoted classifier lines, the first number is the balanced accuracy on sheets left out of training, and "shuffled" is the chance level. "Contour-hinge" results come from the second shape measure, which uses no neural network. Both are described in `notes/results.md`, Data and method.

## Overall result

On the pages Davis gave to Scribe 1, the letter `k` is taller and narrower and has a rounder, larger loop than on the pages of her Scribes 2 and 3. The lines are also further apart. That is the same split as Currier A and B pages. Currier, Davis, Hurych and Timm all agree this difference is visible. The measurements are consistent with it but do not show whether one person or two made it. Beyond that split, Davis's further scribes are not reliably supported by the `k` measurements: Scribes 2 and 3 cannot be told apart, Scribe 3's `k` is not like Scribe 1's as she describes, and her "tall, narrow" Scribe 5 and "oversize loop" Scribe 4 are not found in the measurements. Timm's idea that cramped writing makes smaller letters is consistent with the size difference, since letter height rises with line spacing on every scribe's pages. Some difference in shape remains at equal spacing. Whether one hand wrote the whole book is not tested here.
